Why Singapore

Big-city polish. Tropical ease.

Singapore makes luxury feel effortless. In a single day, you can breakfast at a neighborhood coffee shop, wander through futuristic gardens, cool off beside a rooftop pool and end the night over smoky satay.

This guide mixes the famous sights with slower local moments, so first-time visitors can see the essentials while still leaving room to discover the city.

01 / ICON

Walk Marina Bay at golden hour

Start with Singapore's most cinematic view. Follow the waterfront around Marina Bay - opened in 2010 after an investment of approximately US$5.7 billion, Marina Bay Sands has become one of Singapore's most recognisable landmarks, attracting millions of visitors every year. Then passing the lotus-shaped ArtScience Museum and the skyline before the city lights switch on.

For an elevated evening, pair the walk with a rooftop drink or dinner overlooking the bay. The mood is polished, but the walk itself is wonderfully simple.

Best time · 5:30–8:30 PM
02 / GARDENS

Get lost in Gardens by the Bay

Covering more than 101 hectares, the gardens are home to over 1.5 million plants from around the world.

The Supertree Grove looks like a scene from another planet, while the cooled conservatories offer a break from the tropical heat. Give yourself time rather than rushing between photo spots.

Come in the late afternoon and stay into the evening. You get soft daylight for the gardens and the illuminated Supertrees after dark.

03 / FLAVOUR

Eat your way through a hawker centre

Some of Singapore's most memorable meals come without white tablecloths. Hawker centres bring dozens of specialist stalls together under one roof, making them ideal for trying several local favorites at once.

Look for chicken rice, laksa, satay, kaya toast and fresh sugarcane juice. Go with friends if you can — sharing means more dishes.

04 / WILDLIFE

Spend a morning at Singapore Zoo

Singapore Zoo is one of the city's best escapes when you want a break from skyscrapers and shopping streets. Set amid lush tropical greenery in the Mandai wildlife area, it is known for immersive, open-style habitats and a relaxed atmosphere that makes the visit feel surprisingly removed from the city.

Plan at least half a day and arrive earlier in the morning, when walking is generally more comfortable. Families can easily stay longer, while couples and solo travelers can combine the zoo with another Mandai wildlife experience.

More to love

9 more things worth making time for

Mix these into your Singapore itinerary according to your pace, weather and appetite.

05

Explore Chinatown

Temple architecture, heritage shophouses, tea, street food and contemporary bars make this one of the easiest neighborhoods to explore on foot. Chinatown preserves buildings dating back to the 19th century and is one of Singapore's most historic districts.

06

Wander Kampong Glam

See the golden dome of Sultan Mosque, browse Haji Lane and stop for coffee among colorful murals and independent boutiques.

07

Spend time in Little India

Come for vivid streets, fragrant spice shops, temples and a meal that turns a quick visit into an entire afternoon. Little India is home to one of Singapore's oldest Hindu temples, first established in 1827.

08

Escape to Sentosa

For an easy resort day, head south for beaches, attractions and sunset drinks. It is a useful contrast to the city center. Sentosa welcomes more than 20 million visitors annually and offers over 30 attractions across the island.

09

Visit the Botanic Gardens

Slow the pace among tropical greenery and lawns. The gardens are especially pleasant earlier in the day before the heat builds.

10

See Jewel Changi

If your flight schedule allows, leave time for Jewel's indoor waterfall and greenery — a memorable first or final Singapore stop. The HSBC Rain Vortex stands 40 metres high, making it the world's tallest indoor waterfall.

11

Try a rooftop evening

Dress up a little and see the skyline from above. Reserve around sunset when possible for daylight, blue hour and city lights in one sitting.

12

Take a bumboat ride

See the historic Singapore River from the water and connect the Civic District, Clarke Quay and Marina Bay at an easy pace.

13

Leave room to wander

The best luxury is time. Keep one unscheduled morning for coffee, design shops, architecture or whatever catches your eye.

Travel Planning

How Much Money Do You Need for Singapore?

Singapore is often considered one of the more expensive destinations in Southeast Asia, but the city offers options for almost every type of traveller. With affordable public transport, excellent hawker food, and many free attractions, you can enjoy Singapore without spending a fortune.

A realistic daily budget depends on your travel style. Backpackers can explore Singapore with around S$70–120 per day, while travellers looking for more comfort should expect approximately S$180–300 per day including accommodation, meals, transport, and activities.

Food is one of the easiest ways to control your budget. Singapore's famous hawker centres offer local dishes such as chicken rice, laksa, and satay for around S$5–10 per meal. In comparison, dining at a mid-range restaurant can cost around S$20–50 per person.

Typical Travel Costs in Singapore

Transportation is relatively affordable compared with many major global cities. MRT and bus journeys usually cost around S$1–3, allowing visitors to reach most attractions without taxis. The extensive public transport network includes more than 140 MRT stations, connecting the city centre with neighbourhoods, airports, and tourist areas.

  • Budget accommodation: around S$40–100 per night
  • Mid-range hotels: around S$150–300 per night
  • Hawker centre meal: around S$5–10
  • Café drink: around S$5–8
  • MRT transportation: approximately S$1–3 per journey
  • Major attractions: usually S$30–50 per person

Free Things to Do in Singapore

One of Singapore's biggest advantages is that many memorable experiences are completely free. Visitors can watch the Marina Bay light shows, explore Chinatown and Little India, walk through the UNESCO-listed Singapore Botanic Gardens, visit colourful neighbourhoods, and enjoy waterfront views without paying an entrance fee.

For travellers who plan carefully, Singapore can offer a surprisingly balanced experience: affordable local food during the day, free cultural attractions in the evening, and occasional paid experiences such as Gardens by the Bay, Marina Bay Sands SkyPark, or Sentosa Island.

Quick answers for travelers

Singapore travel FAQ

How many days do you need in Singapore?

Six to seven days works well for a first trip. It gives you time for Marina Bay, cultural neighborhoods, food experiences and either Sentosa or a nature-focused half day.

What are the best things to do in Singapore for first-time visitors?

Start with Marina Bay, Gardens by the Bay, Singapore Zoo, a hawker centre, Chinatown, Kampong Glam and Little India. Add Sentosa, the Botanic Gardens or a rooftop evening depending on your interests.

Is Singapore easy to explore without a car?

Yes. The city is well suited to combining public transport with walking, especially when you group nearby attractions into the same part of your itinerary.

What should I do in Singapore at night?

Walk Marina Bay, see the illuminated Supertrees, eat at a hawker centre, explore the riverfront or book a rooftop drink with skyline views.

Is Singapore Zoo worth visiting?

Yes, especially for families, wildlife lovers and travelers who want a greener break from central Singapore. It is in the Mandai area, so allow around half a day rather than treating it as a quick city-center stop.

What is the best area for a first-time visitor to Singapore?

Marina Bay is convenient for major landmarks, while areas around the Civic District and central MRT connections make it easy to reach Chinatown, Kampong Glam, Little India and other popular parts of Singapore.

Can you see Singapore's main attractions in 3 days?

Yes. A practical 3-day plan is Marina Bay and Gardens by the Bay on day one, heritage neighborhoods and hawker food on day two, then Sentosa or another personal-interest experience on day three.
